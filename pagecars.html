Toyota MR2 AW11

Summary and History

The Toyota MR2 AW11 is the 1.6-liter version of the first-generation MR2, produced between 1984 and 1989. It was one of the first mass-produced Japanese sports cars to combine a mid-engine layout, rear-wheel drive, low weight and a relatively affordable price. The engine was mounted behind the seats, helping the car achieve balanced handling and quick responses through corners.

The AW11 used the famous 4A-GE engine, also found in the Toyota AE86. A more powerful 4A-GZE version later added a supercharger and intercooler. With its pop-up headlights, angular shape and lightweight body, the first MR2 became an icon of the 1980s.

BMW M4 F82

Summary and History

The BMW M4 F82 was produced between 2014 and 2020. It replaced the two-door BMW M3 E92 when BMW separated its performance coupé into the new M4 line. The F82 introduced a modern twin-turbo engine while keeping the rear-wheel-drive character expected from a BMW M car.

Its S55 3.0-liter inline-six produced 431 PS in the standard model and 450 PS with the Competition package. A carbon-fiber roof, muscular bodywork and four exhaust outlets gave the M4 a strong but functional design. Special versions included the Competition, CS and limited-production GTS.

Toyota Supra MK4

Summary and History

The fourth-generation Toyota Supra, known by its A80 chassis code, was produced from 1993 to 2002. Toyota designed it as a grand-touring sports car with a rounded body, rear-wheel drive and the strong 2JZ inline-six engine family.

The naturally aspirated model used the 2JZ-GE, while the Turbo used the legendary 2JZ-GTE with sequential twin turbochargers. Its strong engine, six-speed manual transmission and large tuning potential helped the Supra become one of the most celebrated Japanese sports cars of the 1990s.

Nissan Skyline GT-R R34

Summary and History

The Nissan Skyline GT-R R34 was produced from 1999 to 2002 as the final Skyline GT-R to use the RB26DETT engine. It combined a compact body with advanced technology, including the ATTESA E-TS all-wheel-drive system and a dashboard display that showed live performance information.

Its twin-turbo inline-six was officially rated at 280 PS. The V-Spec models added aerodynamic changes and a more advanced rear differential. Motorsport success, tuning culture and film appearances turned the R34 into a global automotive icon.

Mazda RX-7 FD

Summary and History

The third-generation Mazda RX-7, identified by the FD chassis code, was produced from 1992 to 2002. Its smooth curves, low weight and near-balanced weight distribution made it one of the most admired Japanese sports cars of its era.

Instead of a conventional piston engine, the FD used Mazda's compact 13B-REW twin-rotor engine with sequential twin turbochargers. Later Japanese models reached 280 PS, and the final Spirit R edition combined several of the best features developed during the car's production life.

Porsche 911 Turbo 930

Summary and History

Introduced in 1975, the Porsche 911 Turbo 930 was the first production 911 to use turbocharging. Its wide rear body, large rear spoiler and rear-mounted flat-six engine gave it a shape and driving character unlike anything else on the road.

Early models used a 3.0-liter engine, while the 1978 update brought a 3.3-liter engine, an intercooler and more power. The 930 became famous for strong acceleration and demanding handling. Today it remains one of the most recognizable classic Porsche models.
